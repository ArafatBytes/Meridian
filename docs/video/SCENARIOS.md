# Video scenarios

Six segments, ~3:20. One person, two evenings. Every number here was read off the running system,
not invented — re-check with `verify_video_scenario.py` before each session.

**The through-line:** Nadia commutes home across Dhaka. The app reads the city, she picks a route,
she tells one person, she gets home. A second evening goes worse.

**On-screen text first; narration is being tried on the front half.** Labels are written in the
margin of every segment and carry it alone if the voice is dropped. Segments 0, 1 and 2 now also
have a **voice-over draft, all marked optional.**

**This is now a decision, not a sprinkle.** Three narrated segments followed by four silent ones
is the one outcome to avoid — it reads as a voice-over that gave up. So it is all or nothing:
either narrate 0–6 and the close, or cut every line and let the captions work (they were written
to). The original reason for silence still stands and should be weighed before you record
anything — narration fights the pace at 3:20, and it forces the Bangla/English question that
captions dodge. If you do narrate, English throughout, with the Bangla headlines on screen doing
that work by themselves.

## Segment 0 · Open · 0:10

**Situation.** Nothing yet. This is the only ten seconds in the video that is not a feature —
it buys the viewer a name, a face for the product, and the fact that it is a real app with a
real account behind it.

**Filmed 17 Oct.** One continuous take, no cuts: launch → sign in → loading → Home.

### Start state
- [ ] **Signed out.** Kill the app and clear the session first, or the login screen never appears
- [ ] **A demo account, not your own.** The email is legible in frame for two of these ten
      seconds. Use an address you are happy to publish
- [ ] Password already in the keyboard's autofill, or practised — a mistyped password turns a
      10-second take into a 20-second one
- [ ] Phone on Do Not Disturb, clean background, status bar tidy

### Shots
1. **Phone in frame, app closed.** Tap the icon. Hold half a second on the launch — the app
    icon is the first thing anyone sees of it.
2. **Login.** `Welcome back` · *Sign in to continue*, the shield mark above it. Email and
    password typed at normal speed, not hunted for. Tap **Log in**.
3. **Loading.** The signature animation — the rotating ring, the pulsing shield, four
    satellites — under **Meridian** · *Setting up your safety zone…* Hold the full beat. This
    screen is doing something real (it is resolving the safety zone), and it is the one piece of
    motion in the app that exists nowhere else.
4. **Home.** It settles: the **SOS** button, the Guardian status line, and the four actions —
    *Report crime* · *Plan a route* · *Live map* · *Contacts*. Hold 2 s. Do not tap anything.

### On-screen text
> **MERIDIAN**
> Safety navigation for Dhaka

Place it over shot 1 and clear it before shot 3. The loading screen already carries the wordmark
at display size — a title card on top of it is the same word twice, and the animation is worth
more than the caption.

Nothing else. No problem statement, no feature list: Home states the scope by itself, and
segment 1 opens on the map, which is the problem statement.

### Voice-over (optional — cut candidate)
> Dhaka gets no shortage of warnings. What it doesn't have is anything that tells you which
> street to take tonight.

21 words, ≈8 s at narration pace, which leaves the last beat silent on Home. Alternate, if the
cut runs long and you want 5 s instead:

> Dhaka doesn't lack warnings. It lacks something that tells you which street to take tonight.

**Why it is marked optional.** The rest of the video has no narration (see the note at the top),
so a voice here either commits the whole piece to a voice or reads as an abandoned idea. Two ways
to keep it honest: narrate segment 0 and the close only, framed as bookends — or cut it and let
the title card do the work. Decide before scoring the edit, not after.

### Editor
This is the segment to **cut hardest**. Ten seconds of filling in a login form is ten seconds of
nothing happening, and it is the cheapest ten seconds in the video to lose. Speed shots 1–2 to
~2× so the typing reads as *an app with accounts* rather than *watch me type*, keep shot 3 at
1× (the animation is the only thing here anyone will remember), and hold shot 4 long enough to
read the four action labels.

If the whole video overruns, this segment goes first — drop to the loading screen and Home, 4 s,
and the video loses nothing it was making an argument with.

## Segment 4 · Nobody knows where I am · 0:45

**Situation.** She sets off, tells her sister, and then stops answering. Thirteen real minutes,
cut to forty-five seconds.

**This is the segment the judges asked for.** It is the only one where the app *does* something
rather than shows something, and it carries six features in one unbroken take: the deadline, the
one chosen contact, live navigation on a moving dot, the ladder escalating by itself, the answer,
and arrival. Three emails land on a laptop while it runs. Nothing in it is faked except the GPS.

### The moving dot, and what GPX is

A **GPX file** is a plain text list of latitude/longitude points in order — a breadcrumb trail.
Ours is `backend/data/video/FILM-Rampura-to-Gulshan2.gpx`: **178 points, 5.49 km**, exported
straight out of the route the app recommended, so the moving dot and the blue line are the same
geometry and the dot cannot drift off the road at a junction.

**Nothing to install.** Android ships a test location provider in the shell, and
`play_mock_track.py` drives it over adb:

```
backend\venv\Scripts\python.exe backend\scripts\play_mock_track.py
```

It resamples the track to one fix a second at 25 km/h — **793 fixes, 13.2 minutes** — because
OSRM spaces its points by turns, not by time (3 m apart at junctions, 195 m on a flyover), and
replaying those raw makes the dot crawl and then leap. The last fix lands **11.5 m** from the
destination, inside `ARRIVAL_M` of 30 m, so **navigation ends by itself** on camera.

It drives **three** location providers at once — `fused`, `gps` and `network` — and that is not
belt-and-braces, it is the whole thing working. `expo-location` asks Play Services' fused client,
which *blends* the platform providers. Mock only `fused` and `network` keeps reporting the real
phone every few seconds; the app's first fix on `watchPositionAsync` can be that one, navigation
starts kilometres off its own route, the off-route timer trips, and it reroutes from where the
phone really is. On camera that reads as the dot teleporting mid-journey and sticking there.

**Check before you press Start:**

```
adb shell "dumpsys location | grep -E 'fused|gps|network' | grep -m3 'last location'"
```

All three lines must end in `mock` and agree with each other. If any of them still shows your
real position, Start will reroute on you.

The app has no `isFromMockProvider` check anywhere, so an injected fix is treated exactly like a
real one: the route, the dot, the ladder, the arrival and all three emails run their true code
paths. The script removes the provider when it exits, Ctrl+C included. If a run dies hard,
`backend\venv\Scripts\python.exe
backend\scripts\play_mock_track.py --teardown` puts the phone back on its own GPS.

### Start state
- [ ] **The worker must be RUNNING.** `journey_watch` is scheduled `* * * * *` in the same worker
      as `live_risk`, and it *is* the ladder — with the worker stopped nothing escalates and this
      segment cannot be filmed at all. `docker compose up -d worker`, then `docker compose ps`
- [ ] `CHECK_IN_WINDOW` is **1 minute** on this branch, which is what makes the ladder filmable
- [ ] One emergency contact with a reachable inbox (3 exist, all with email)
- [ ] Inbox open on a laptop, in the same frame or as a cutaway, first mail already *Not spam*
- [ ] Phone plugged in, unlocked, and showing up in `adb devices`
- [ ] Push permission granted; Do Not Disturb except Meridian

### The order to do it in — this part matters

**One continuous phone recording, about sixteen minutes.** Nothing is paused and nothing is
re-taken; every cut happens in the edit. The three emails are captured separately, because they
are on a laptop and cannot appear in a phone screen recording.

**Before you press record**

1. `docker compose up -d worker`, then `docker compose ps` and actually see it *Up*.
2. Start the track:

   ```
   backend\venv\Scripts\python.exe backend\scripts\play_mock_track.py
   ```

   It parks the phone at Rampura and waits for Enter. It waits indefinitely, so there is no
   clock on you.
3. In Meridian, plan `Rampura` -> `Gulshan 2` as in segment 3, first suggestion each time. Leave
   the route card on screen. **Do not touch the deadline chip and do not press Start yet** —
   those are shots 1 and 3 and they have to be inside the recording.

**Record**

4. Start the phone screen recording.
5. Tap the **2 min** deadline chip — shot 1.
6. Tap the contact under **If you go quiet, tell**, hold on the line it reveals — shot 2.
7. Press **Start** — shot 3.
8. Switch to the terminal and press **Enter**. The dot begins to move.

**Then leave it alone and do exactly two things**

| From Start | What happens by itself | You |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | grey *"started a journey"* email sent | nothing |
| 2:00–3:00 | card retitles itself **"Are you there yet?"**, grows **I'm fine** | nothing — let it sit |
| 3:00–5:00 | **"Asked twice, still no answer."** | nothing |
| 5:00–6:00 | guardian emailed — the red one | nothing |
| ~6:30 | — | **tap I'm fine** — shot 9 |
| 13:12 | dot arrives, navigation ends itself, green email | nothing |
| ~13:30 | — | **stop recording** |

The two taps in that whole stretch are **I'm fine** and **stop**. Everything else is the app
escalating on its own, which is the point of the segment.

Answering buys fifteen minutes (`ANSWER_EXTENSION`) and arrival is seven minutes later, so the
ladder cannot fire a second time and spoil the end.

**Afterwards.** The script holds the final fix for 60 s, then removes the test provider itself.
If you killed it instead, run it again with `--teardown`.

**Do not use `--stop-after` for this take.** Escalation is driven by an unanswered check-in, not
by the dot halting, and a frozen dot never arrives — it would cost you shots 10 and 11. It
exists only for a separate insert.

### Captured separately — the three emails

Shots **4, 8 and 11** are the inbox on your laptop. Grab each one as it lands, not at the end:

| Shot | Email | Lands | Capture |

| 8 | red · *"Nadia has not arrived and is not answering"* | 5–6 min | **screen-record** — hold on the last line, *"You are the only person who has been told."* |
| 11 | green · *"Nadia arrived safely"* | ~13 min | a still is enough |

### Shots
1. On the route card, tap the **2 min** deadline chip. The *Check in by* line jumps to a clock
   time two minutes out.
2. Under **If you go quiet, tell**, tap the contact. Hold on the line that appears:
   *"They get an email when you set off, when you arrive, and if you stop answering check-ins."*
3. **Start.** Navigation begins and the dot moves along the route.
4. Cutaway: the inbox. **Slate-grey** email — *"Nadia has started a journey"*. Scroll it: **no
   location anywhere.**
5. Back to the phone. The journey card counts down to the check-in. Hold.
6. The **journey card** changes its own title to **"Are you there yet?"** and grows an **I'm

7. Second nudge. Card reads **"Asked twice, still no answer."** and *"If this stays unanswered,

8. Cutaway: the **red** email — *"Nadia has not arrived and is not answering"*. Hold on the last

### On-screen text
> Tell one person. Once.
> **Nothing shared while everything is fine** — no location in this email
> No answer → it asks
> Still no answer → **her sister is told where she was last**

### Editor
- Film the **journey card**, not the lock-screen notification. `JG2.md` records the push banner

- Speed the dot's travel to 4–8×. Title-card the waits: *4 minutes later*.

## Pre-flight, both sessions

```
backend\venv\Scripts\python.exe backend\scripts\stage_video.py
```

**Use the venv's Python, not a bare `python`.** On this machine a bare `python` is the base
conda environment, which has no `asyncpg`, and the failure reads as a missing dependency when
nothing is missing. The script checks this first and prints the right command, but it is one
less thing to hit at 20:55.

That one command seeds the surface (including the replayed real news), lists the real flags
segment 2 can tap, verifies the routing contrast and names the pair to film. It ends on
**READY TO FILM** or **NOT READY**. If it reports no real news flags, see PLAN.md T10.

Then by hand:

- [ ] **Ingestion: stop it for segment 3 only, and put it back.** `docker compose stop worker`
      freezes the flag surface, because `live_risk` runs every fifteen minutes and a real flag
      landing on a recommended corridor breaks segment 3 — it did at 19:40 on 17 Oct, on the
      Rampura corridor. **But `journey_watch` lives in that same worker on a one-minute cadence
      and it is segment 4's entire ladder, and `alert_dispatch` is in there too.** So:
      `docker compose up -d worker` **before segment 1** (the risk badge then reads *news 4 min
      ago* rather than hours) and **before segment 4**, without exception. If you only remember
      one thing: leave the worker up, and re-run the verifier instead
- [ ] Hot word set, and **not a single common word**. It is `Help` as of 17 Oct, which
      `phraseWasSaid` matches as a whole word — so it fires on "this will help you" mid-
      narration. A two- or three-word phrase cannot: the matcher needs the whole sequence
- [ ] Inbox open on a laptop, first email already marked *Not spam*
- [ ] Phone visible to `adb devices`. **No mock-location app needed** — segment 4 uses
      `backend/scripts/play_mock_track.py`, which drives Android's own test provider over adb
- [ ] Phone on Do Not Disturb except Meridian
- [ ] One `live-%` flag confirmed present, and you know which one you are tapping
