## What this proves

**S18's hot-word half, and the end of a dead loop that has been in this codebase
since before the relay started.**

`sos_hot_words` has been written by the app and **read by nothing** —
`migrations/supabase/README.md` carried it as a known defect, and
`/voice/analyze` made it worse by ignoring the stored phrases in favour of a
hardcoded list and returning an empty transcript unconditionally, so `trigger`
was always `false`. The screen looked like a feature and was a form.

Now: you store a phrase, you arm the listener, you say the phrase, and **SOS
fires** — with `voice_activated` finally recorded on the incident, which
`sos_service.py` had been carrying as `voice_activated=False  # the voice path
sets this once S18 lands`.

Three things worth asking about:

- **Nothing is uploaded.** Recognition happens on the device, and the app
  **refuses to listen at all** on a phone that cannot do that — it does not
  quietly fall back to network recognition. That refusal is checked by a test
  that reads the source, because the failure is a *missing option*, not a wrong
  value, and no running app could show you the difference.
- **The matcher is strict on purpose.** Whole-word matching for Latin scripts,
  substring for Bangla, a four-character minimum, and a refusal to store a phrase
  that an existing one would shadow. A false positive here emails somebody's
  family to say they are in danger.
- **One audio upload path was deleted, not ported.** The microphone button on
  this screen used to record a clip and post it to `/ai/speech-to-text` to fill
  in a text field. It dictates on-device now, and
  `services/speech.service.ts` + `hooks/useVoiceRecorder.ts` are gone.

## What this is NOT — say this plainly if anyone asks

**It is not an always-on wake word.** The microphone opens when you tap **Arm
voice SOS** and closes on a match, on disarm, on an error, or after **ten
minutes**. It does not listen when the app is closed, it does not listen in the
background, and it does not survive leaving the screen.

That is a deliberate narrowing. Always-on detection is what people expect from
the phrase "hot words", and shipping it would mean an open microphone whenever
the app runs, a measurable battery cost and an Android foreground-service
notification. Arming before you walk home is a smaller promise the
implementation actually keeps — and the panel says so in the app, in words, above
the button.

## Steps

### Step 0 · The app opens in Expo Go, and says why the feature is missing

Do this one **in Expo Go**, before building anything. It takes a minute and it is
the check that matters most, because the failure it guards against is not a
broken feature — it is a broken app.

Open the app in Expo Go. **Expect it to open normally**, all tabs working.

❌ **If you get `[runtime not ready]: Error: Cannot find native module
'ExpoSpeechRecognition'`**, the lazy-loading in `src/features/voice/recognizer.ts`
has been undone. Someone has added a static
`import ... from 'expo-speech-recognition'` somewhere in `src/features/voice/`.
`npx jest src/features/voice/privacy.test.ts` names the file.

Then: Profile → **SOS Hot Words**. Add `help me` (see step 1 for the validation
rules). The **Voice SOS** panel appears with an amber line:

> *Voice SOS needs the Meridian development build — Expo Go cannot load the
> speech recogniser. Everything else in the app works; use the SOS button.*

**Arm voice SOS** is disabled. Tap **Dictate** — **expect** *"Cannot dictate on
this phone"* naming the development build, and the text field still typeable.

That is the whole point of the fix: one more reason the device cannot listen,
reported the same way as the on-device refusal, instead of an app that will not
start.

### Step 1 · The screen refuses a bad phrase, with a reason

Profile → **SOS Hot Words**.

Type `sos` and tap **Add Hot Word**.

**Expect:** *"Cannot use that phrase — Too short — use at least 4 characters.
Very short phrases are set off by ordinary speech and background noise."*

The old check was `!newHotWord.trim()`, which accepted this. Three characters is
not a safety margin: "bus", "box" and "bas" are one phoneme apart and all
plausible on a Dhaka street, so a three-letter trigger fires on traffic.

Now add `help me`. **Expect** it to save.

Then try to add `help me now`.

**Expect:** *"\"help me\" is already in this phrase, so it would trigger first.
This one would never be used."*

That is the one worth pausing on. The user's mental model is that more phrases
means more coverage. It does not — `help me` matches first and fires, so
`help me now` would be an entry that can never be reached. Storing it silently
would leave you believing you had configured something you had not.

### Step 2 · The panel tells you what this phone can actually do

Scroll to the top. The **Voice SOS** panel appears now that there is a phrase to
listen for.

**Expect** the explanatory line: *"Meridian listens for your phrase **only while
armed**, for up to 10 minutes, and **only on this phone** — no audio leaves the
device."*

Then one of three states:

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Arm voice SOS** enabled, no warning | on-device recognition is available and a language pack is installed | go to step 3 |
| Amber: *"No offline language pack is installed…"* plus **Install an offline language pack** | Android 13+, pack missing | tap it, finish the system dialog, come back, tap **Arm** |
| Amber: *"This phone cannot recognise speech without sending audio to a server, so Meridian will not listen. Use the SOS button instead."* | no on-device recogniser | **this is a pass.** See below |

The third row is the step's real content. It is the product refusing to do the
thing it advertises rather than doing it badly — `requiresOnDeviceRecognition` is
**silently ignored** on a device that cannot honour it, so without that refusal a
feature described as private would be streaming the microphone to a third party
with nothing to say so. S18's instruction was *"do not ship a fake"*, and the
manual SOS button is right there, which is the fallback it asks for.

If you get the third row, steps 3–6 cannot run on this phone. Note it on the
sign-off and test on a different handset; everything else is still verifiable.

### Step 3 · Arm it

Tap **Arm voice SOS**. Grant microphone permission if asked.

**Expect:**

- the button turns red and reads **Stop listening**;
- a countdown appears top-right, starting near **10:00**;
- a box appears: **LISTENING · en-US · ON THIS DEVICE** and *"Say your phrase…"*.

### Step 4 · It hears you, and shows what it heard

Say something that is **not** your phrase — "the weather is fine today".

**Expect** the box to fill in with what you said, in quotes, and **no trigger**.

That line is doing two jobs. It proves recognition is working at all, which
matters for accents and street noise. And it is the only way to see that the
transcript stays on the phone — it is rendered, never sent.

Let it sit for thirty seconds without speaking. **Expect it to stay armed.** The
recogniser ends its own session on a long silence and the hook restarts it; if
"armed" stopped meaning armed after the first pause, the feature would be
useless and nothing on screen would say so.

### Step 5 · Say the phrase

Say **"help me"**.

**Expect** a dialog: **Voice SOS triggered** — *Heard "help me".* with the
transcript, and two buttons: **Cancel** and **Send SOS**.

Tap **Cancel**.

**Expect** the panel to be **disarmed** — the microphone closes the moment a
phrase matches, before the dialog appears.

The confirmation step is deliberate. A recogniser mishearing a phrase and sending
an SOS to somebody's family with no chance to stop it is a worse failure than a
two-second delay.

Now test the false-positive guard. Arm again and say **"that was helpful"**.

**Expect no trigger.** `helpful` contains `help`, but your phrase is `help me`
and Latin matching is on whole words. Add `help` as a second phrase and try
"that was helpful" again — **still no trigger**, because `helpful` is one word
and `help` is another.

### Step 6 · A hot word fires the real SOS

Arm, say **"help me"**, and this time tap **Send SOS**.

**Expect** the normal SOS flow — the camera opens, you record, the progress bar
runs, contacts are emailed. The same path the red button uses; there is one SOS
in this app and this is it.

### Step 7 · The incident records that it was your voice

Profile → **SOS History**.

**Expect** the newest entry to show it was voice-activated.

```powershell
docker compose exec api python -c @'
import asyncio, logging; logging.disable(logging.INFO)
from sqlalchemy import text
from app.core.database import AsyncSessionLocal, engine
async def main():
    async with AsyncSessionLocal() as db:
        for r in (await db.execute(text("""
            select timestamp, voice_activated, status
            from public.sos_incidents
            order by timestamp desc limit 3"""))):
            print(r)
    await engine.dispose()
asyncio.run(main())
'@
```

**Expect `voice_activated = True`** on the row you just created, and `False` on
any earlier one.

This column has existed since the first migration with
`voice_activated=False,  # the voice path sets this once S18 lands` written
beside it in `sos_service.py`. That comment is gone.

### Step 8 · Dictation happens on the device too

Tap **Dictate** next to the phrase field and say **"save me"**.

**Expect** the field to fill in with what you said, and **no stop button and no
duration counter** — on-device dictation ends by itself when you stop speaking.

The old button recorded a clip with `expo-audio`, uploaded it to
`/ai/speech-to-text`, and needed a stop control and a 30-second cap to bound a
recording that only existed in order to be uploaded. The endpoint behind it does
not even work — **defect 2**, `pydub` import commented out, still open, and now
with one fewer caller.

If this phone has no on-device recogniser you will get *"Cannot dictate on this
phone — … Type the phrase instead."* Also a pass: typing was always the primary
path here.

### Step 9 · Leaving the screen closes the microphone

Arm it, then tap back to the profile menu.

**Expect** the microphone indicator in the Android status bar to disappear
immediately.

Then come back into the screen. **Expect it to be disarmed**, not still running.
An armed recogniser surviving a navigation transition is how a microphone stays
open after the feature is finished with it.

### Step 10 · The session expires

Arm it and leave it for ten minutes without speaking.

**Expect** it to disarm itself and say *"Stopped listening after 10 minutes. Arm
it again if you need it."*

Slow, and worth doing once. An armed session that never expires is an open
microphone the user has forgotten about.

### Step 11 · `/voice/analyze` is gone

```powershell
curl.exe -s -o NUL -w "%{http_code}`n" -X POST http://localhost:8000/api/v1/voice/analyze
```

**Expect `404`.**

S18 said *"`/voice/analyze` either works against real hot words or is removed"*,
and removal is the right half: matching happens on the device, so an endpoint for
it would mean uploading audio — the thing the privacy bar exists to prevent.

### Step 12 · The automated checks

```powershell
cd D:\Meridian\frontend
npx jest src/features/voice
```

**Expect 66 passed.** The ones that matter:

- `privacy.test.ts` reads the voice feature's own source and asserts every
  `start()` call passes `requiresOnDeviceRecognition: true`, that
  `supportsOnDeviceRecognition()` is checked as a **guard**, that no recording is
  persisted, and that nothing in the feature imports an HTTP client. Behaviour
  tests cannot check any of that — the recogniser is native and the difference
  between on-device and network recognition is invisible from JavaScript.
- `hotwords.test.ts` covers the matcher in both directions, with the
  false-positive cases as the longer list.
- `recognizer.test.ts` mocks the package into throwing on require — which is
  exactly what Expo Go does — and asserts the feature degrades into a message
  rather than a dereference error. It is the only way to exercise that path on a
  laptop.
- `privacy.test.ts`'s last block asserts **no file statically imports the
  package** and that nothing uses `useSpeechRecognitionEvent` (the package's own
  hook, which imports it statically). Both would reintroduce the boot crash.

## Known limits

- **Android 13+ only, in practice.** On-device recognition and continuous mode
  both need it. On Android 12 and below `getSupportedLocales` returns nothing and
  the panel refuses to arm.
- **Bangla depends on the device.** `bn-BD` and `bn-IN` are in the preferred
  locale list and will be used if installed, but Google's on-device packs for
  Bengali are not available on every handset. The matcher handles Bangla
  correctly (substring, for the agglutinative suffixes); whether the *recogniser*
  does is the phone's business, not ours. **Check this before promising Bangla
  voice SOS to anyone.**
- **iOS is untested.** The code paths exist and `supportsOnDeviceRecognition`
  gates them the same way. Nothing has been run on an iPhone — do not claim it
  works.
- **No always-on wake word.** By design; see *What this is NOT*. If that is
  wanted, it is a different feature with a foreground service and a battery
  budget, and it belongs in its own sprint.
- **`voice_activated` is the client's own claim.** A modified client could assert
  it falsely. That is acceptable because nothing is authorised on the flag — it
  changes one boolean on the history screen, not who gets emailed. Treat it as
  provenance, not as a credential.
